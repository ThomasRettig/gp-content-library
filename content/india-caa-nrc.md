## Summary

The amendment of the CAA removes barriers for acquiring Indian citizenship for some religions. Many other groups in South Asia region such as Rohingya Muslims, Sri Lankan Tamils, Bhutanese, Hazaras, Shias and Ahmadiyyas face exclusion without justification by this amendment.

The proposed National Register of Citizens (NRC) meanwhile, is a more inclusive state-driven bureaucratic exercise designed to compile a definitive register of all legal Indian citizens by demanding stringent historical proof of lineage and residency. It has only been implemented in the state of Assam.

## Personal reflection

This is tragic for the marginalized. In rural India, most people are borned without a proper birth certificate. There is a disconnect between people living in cities and rural areas; they do not care that rural areas lack these official documentations and people will not have proper citizenship despite living their entire life in this country. Current system is enforcing rules that were non-existent decades ago. For example, an impoverished woman, already disenfranchised by structural patriarchy, must somehow produce paternal lineage documents she never had access to, just to prove she belongs to the only home she has ever known, which is simply ridiculous. This had led to millions of vulnerable individuals who do not have a country they belong to and forced to live in poverty, even though it is not their fault.

## Essay question applicability

1. **"The best test of a civilised society is the way in which it treats its weakest members." (1996)**
* **How it supports:** The India CAA-NRC case study highlights how a **"strict bureaucratic filter"** penalises **"documentation poverty"** among the rural poor and female demographics, who represent the "weakest members" vulnerable to mass statelessness.
* **Limitation/counterpoint:** From a governance perspective, the state might argue that its primary "Responsibility" is to address the **Physiological and Safety needs** of its legal citizens. This necessitates registries for national security and resource management, prioritizing the survival of the established collective over the inclusion of the undocumented.



2. **"In the global age we inhabit, there is no justification for national boundaries." (2018)**
* **How it supports:** This relates to the **Globalisation & Borders** theme, where the CAA-NRC represents an **"aggressive reassertion of Westphalian state sovereignty"** that contradicts the ideal of an interconnected, borderless world.
* **Limitation/counterpoint:** The **Natural Factors** of governance, such as **power asymmetries** and the instinct for **national survival**, provide a structural justification for boundaries. Borders serve as a necessary tool to prevent perceived threats to social stability and to manage finite national resources.


3. **"A leader's responsibility should always be to his or her own country, not other nations." (2019)**
* **How it supports:** The CAA’s **"selective safety net"** for specific non-Muslim minorities tests whether a leader's **"duty of care"** should be strictly nationalistic and majoritarian or if it should adhere to universal standards of **Human Rights and Identity**.
* **Limitation/counterpoint:** This "absolute" claim is challenged by **Transcendence needs** at the state level. One could argue that true national leadership includes **"global altruism"** and the promotion of global justice, as a country’s moral standing is a form of soft power that serves its long-term interests.


4. **"Can prejudice ever be eliminated?" (2011)**
* **How it supports:** The case study details how **"majoritarian politics"** and **"structural asymmetry"** create humanitarian crises, suggesting that prejudice can be formalised into law through demographic registries, making it an institutional fixture rather than just a social one.
* **Limitation/counterpoint:** Eliminating prejudice is an **"Achievability"** challenge that may be deemed impossible if the root causes are **Natural** human instincts for tribalism. If prejudice is rooted in **Cultural shared values** that resist secular integration, then legal frameworks like the CAA are symptoms of an inescapable human condition rather than a surmountable policy error.


5. **"Is it justifiable for nations to always prioritise their national interests?" (2018)**
* **How it supports:** The NRC acts as a tool for **"state control and profiling,"** where the "national interest" of defining legal citizenship creates a **"structural and humanitarian crisis"** for marginalised groups who lack the means to prove their status.
* **Limitation/counterpoint:** While prioritising national interests is a **Normative** duty of a government, doing so "always" is unjustifiable if it violates the **secular foundations** of the nation’s own Constitution. There is a "Doubt" complication here: if a state destroys its own core values to protect its "interests," it may no longer be the nation it set out to defend.
